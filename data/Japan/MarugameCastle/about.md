# Title: Marugame Castle, November 2025

My son-in-law and older grandson took a train trip to see Marugame Castle, which is about
an hour away from where they live.

It is designated a National Historic Site of Japan, and an Important Cultural Property.

## IDG_20251110_115224_842.jpeg

An informational sign. English translation (from Google Translate, possibly correctly):

National Important Cultural Property Designated on June 9, 1943

The keep, located on the northern side of the main enclosure at the highest point of Marugame Castle, is one of only 12 existing wooden keeps in Japan that retain their original form from the time of construction. The construction date is believed to be between 1643, when the Yamazaki clan began construction of Marugame Castle, and 1660, based on the fact that a large tower is already depicted in the current keep's location in the "Shoho Castle Map" submitted to the shogunate in 1645, and that a prayer tablet with the year 1660 inscribed in ink was found inside the wall of the third floor during the dismantling and repair work carried out from 1948 to 1950. Originally, the keep and the turrets built at the four corners of the main enclosure were connected by connecting turrets, surrounding the main enclosure.

The structure is approximately 15 meters high, with three stories and three floors, and is built in the style of a boat house. The exterior walls and eaves are entirely covered with fire-resistant plaster. The first floor is 6 ken (approximately 10.8 meters) east-west and 5 ken (approximately 9 meters) north-south, with weatherboard siding up to about 2 meters high on the north, east, and south sides, excluding the west side. In addition to a stone drop, the north side also features iron-barred loopholes and gun loopholes, strengthening the defense of the back entrance passage leading from the third enclosure to the second enclosure, which passes under the north side of the keep. The second floor is 4.5 ken (approximately 8.1 meters) east-west and 3.5 ken (approximately 6.3 meters) north-south, with curved gables on both the north and south sides, and lattice windows placed on either side of the gables. The third floor is 3 ken (approximately 5.4 meters) east-west and 2 ken (approximately 3.6 meters) north-south, with curved gables on both the east and west sides, and a ventilation device on the north side.

Although the keep's size, with the widest first floor being 6 ken east-west and 5 ken north-south, is the smallest among the 12 existing wooden keeps, the skillful arrangement of decorative elements such as curved gables and triangular gables on each floor creates a dignified appearance despite its small size.
